LOCAL · The Housing Market

Orlando homes for sale hit their highest level of 2026 as the market nears balance

November 20, 2026

Central Florida’s housing shelf is the fullest it has been all year. The Orlando Regional REALTOR® Association’s October 2026 report puts the number of homes for sale at 12,762 — the highest inventory recorded in 2026, up from 12,464 in September. Measured the way the market measures balance, that is 5.8 months of supply: how long the homes now listed would take to sell at the recent pace of buying if nothing new came onto the market. Six months is the level the association itself calls balanced, so Greater Orlando is close to that line without crossing into a buyer’s market.

Prices eased a little further while choice grew. The median sold price in October was $398,719 — the middle of what homes actually sold for — down slightly from $399,610 in September and 2.9% below the $410,543 recorded in October 2025. The split by property type matters: 1,713 single-family homes sold at a median of $436,289, while 472 condos and townhouses sold at a median of $289,680. In plain words, the typical house and the typical condo are living in different price worlds, and each should be judged against its own numbers.

The pace of the market slowed without stalling. Overall, 2,185 homes sold in October, down 6.0% from September, and new listings fell 5.0% to 3,370 as some would-be sellers waited. Homes spent an average of 66 days on the market, unchanged from September. The association’s 2026 president, Chris Atwell, reads the month as Central Florida following a national trend: inventory is giving buyers more choices and more room to negotiate, while mortgage rates sit nearly a full percentage point higher than they were a year ago.

For a Central Florida homeowner, the plain reading is that this market rewards honesty. With 12,762 homes competing for the same buyers, a price set from what similar nearby homes actually sold for is what gets a home its showings; a hopeful price sits, and 66 days is now the average wait even for homes that do sell. For a buyer or an investor, near-balance is working room: time to inspect properly, room to negotiate on price or terms, and no need to waive the homework out of fear. The deal still has to work at today’s rate on the day it closes — but in a market this close to balanced, patience is a real negotiating tool again.

Source: Orlando Regional REALTOR® Association (ORRA) housing report, data via Stellar MLS — October 2026

UNITED STATES · Interest Rates

Mortgage rates jump back up: the 30-year fixed climbs to 7.28% and ends a four-week slide

November 20, 2026

The four-week slide in mortgage rates is over. Freddie Mac’s weekly Primary Mortgage Market Survey, released Thursday, November 19, 2026, puts the average 30-year fixed rate at 7.28% for the week ending November 19 — up 17 basis points from 7.11% the week before. The 15-year fixed averaged 6.66%, up from 6.45%. A year ago, the 30-year average stood at 6.26%, so today’s buyer is paying roughly a full percentage point more than last November’s buyer for the same loan.

The reason sits outside housing itself. The October Consumer Price Index came in hotter than economists expected, and reporting on the release says markets now expect the Federal Reserve to raise its benchmark rate again at its December 8 meeting — the debate has shifted from whether the Fed hikes to how aggressively it moves. That expectation pushed the 10-year Treasury yield back toward 5%, and mortgage rates tend to follow that yield. In plain words: inflation is the lever, and until it cools, rate relief keeps getting postponed.

The strain is showing elsewhere in the same week’s reporting. Pending home sales fell to a record low in October, existing-home sales hit their lowest level since 2010, and the National Association of Home Builders reported builder confidence at a new 14-year low, with permit activity at its weakest since the early pandemic. Those are national figures, not Orlando figures — but they describe the weather system every local market is standing in: fewer signed contracts, cautious builders, and a supply pipeline under pressure.

For a Central Florida buyer, the working lesson is the one Freddie Mac’s own chief economist points to: shop the loan the way you shop the house. Comparing quotes from several lenders can save thousands over the life of a loan, and that saving is available today, at today’s rates, without waiting for the Fed. For a seller, a 7.28% world means your buyer’s budget is set by the payment, not by your asking price — price to the payment math a real buyer can carry, and the home can still move in a market where well-priced homes are closing every week.

Source: Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey, reported by Realtor.com — November 19, 2026

LOCAL · The Housing Market

Nearly one in four Orlando listings has cut its price as asking prices slip

November 20, 2026

Asking prices in Orlando are bending, and the clearest sign is how many sellers have already lowered them. In Realtor.com’s October 2026 reading of the Orlando market, 23.7% of active listings — nearly one in four — carried a price cut. That share sits above the national figure of 20.5%, which means Orlando sellers are adjusting more often than sellers in the country as a whole. A price cut is a seller saying, in public, that the first number was a hope and the market has answered it.

The asking-price level itself is slipping. Orlando’s median list price was $405,000 in October, down 3.6% from a year earlier — a steeper drop than the national decline of 2.2%, and just under the national median list price of $415,000. Choice is part of the story: 14,090 homes were actively listed, up 2.8% from a year ago. That is a smaller build in supply than the national increase of 7.4%, so Orlando is not flooded — but a buyer here still has more homes to compare than a year ago, and sellers can feel that in every negotiation.

One number in this report needs a careful read. Realtor.com puts Orlando’s median time on market at 78 days, while the Orlando Regional REALTOR® Association’s October report puts the average at 66 days. Those are different yardsticks — a median of currently listed homes against an average of homes that sold, measured by two different organizations — so they will not match, and neither one is wrong. Each story on this page credits its own source and its own measure, and this is why: the honest habit is to read every figure with the name of the organization that produced it.

For a homeowner thinking of selling, the plain meaning is that the first price is a decision, not a formality. In a market where nearly one listing in four has already cut once, starting at the price the sold evidence supports is how a home avoids becoming next month’s price-cut statistic. For a buyer, a cut listing is not a damaged listing — it is often simply a home whose seller started hopeful and has now met the market. That is exactly the kind of listing where a fair, well-documented offer has its best chance.

Source: Realtor.com October 2026 housing data for Orlando — November 2026

WORLDWIDE · Worldwide Housing

Canada’s housing market holds steady in October while prices stay below last year

November 20, 2026

Canada’s housing market spent October standing still — and in a year of falling prices, standing still counts as news. The Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA), releasing its October 2026 figures on November 17, reported that home sales recorded over Canadian MLS® Systems edged up 0.3% from September. That is the smallest of increases, and it follows months of decline: a market pausing rather than turning.

Underneath the steady month, the year-over-year picture is still soft. Actual sales totalled 37,827 homes in October, down 10.6% from October 2025. The national average sale price was $678,584 in Canadian dollars, down 1.6% from a year earlier, and CREA’s MLS® Home Price Index — its like-for-like measure of what the same kind of home sells for — was down 3.1% from a year ago. In plain words: roughly the same number of homes changed hands as the month before, for a little less money than a year ago.

Supply, meanwhile, looks ordinary rather than stressed. About 197,000 properties were listed for sale across Canadian MLS® Systems at the end of October — slightly above the long-term average of 190,000 — amounting to about five months of inventory, right on its long-term average. That is the same neighborhood Greater Orlando is in, with its 5.8 months of supply: neither a rush nor a glut, but a market where buyers can take their time and sellers have to earn the sale.

The lesson travels the same way it did from Hong Kong last week, in the opposite direction. A famous market going flat is not a reading of what a house in Orlando, Kissimmee, or Sanford is worth — Canada’s prices move on Canadian rates, Canadian incomes, and Canadian supply. What does apply here is the pattern worth recognizing: in more than one country this fall, housing has settled into the same shape — prices a little below last year, supply near balance, and neither side able to take the other for granted. Judged by local sold prices and local supply, that is a workable market, not a broken one.

Source: Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA) — November 17, 2026

LOCAL · The Housing Market

Orlando housing market close to balanced as sellers close at about 96% of asking

November 11, 2026

Greater Orlando is sitting close to balanced — the middle ground between a buyer’s market and a seller’s market. The measure that shows it is months of supply: 4.8 months. In plain words, that is how long the homes currently for sale would take to sell at the recent pace of buying if no new homes came onto the market. At 4.8 months, neither side holds a clear advantage: sellers cannot count on a rush of competing buyers, and buyers cannot count on desperate sellers.

The price numbers tell the same steady story. Over the 12 months through September 2026, the median sold price in Greater Orlando was $397,000 — the middle of what homes actually sold for, not what they were first listed at. The typical seller closed at about 96% of the original asking price, which means the first list price is best understood as a starting point for negotiation, not the final number. And homes are still changing hands in real numbers: 2,812 homes closed across the four counties in October, so this is a market that is moving, not one that has frozen.

What this means in plain words for a Central Florida homeowner: the market will reward a fair price and punish a hopeful one. When supply sits near balance, an asking price set from what similar nearby homes actually sold for gives a home its best chance to move, while a price set well above the sold evidence tends to sit — and a home that sits invites lower offers later. Condition and presentation matter more in a balanced market too, because buyers have other homes to choose from and compare against yours on the same weekend.

For an investor, a balanced market is a numbers market. There is no rising tide doing the work for you, so a purchase has to make sense on the day it closes: the price paid, the work the property honestly needs, and the rent or resale the real numbers support. The opportunity in this kind of market is patience — sellers closing at about 96% of asking are showing, in the open, that a well-supported offer below asking can still get a deal done.

Source: Stellar MLS data reported by The Homes in Orlando Team — November 2026

WORLDWIDE · Worldwide Housing

Hong Kong luxury homes surge while much of the world cools

November 10, 2026

Hong Kong’s luxury housing market is running hot while much of the world’s housing cools. Luxury home prices there are up 5.3% so far this year through September 2026. That is not a one-month blip: prices now stand 11.3% above their March 2025 low, which means the top of that market has been climbing back, step by step, for well over a year — a recovery at the very top end even while ordinary housing in many countries stays quiet.

The sales count shows how strong the top end is. Between January and September, 197 homes sold for more than HK$100 million each — up 109.6% from a year earlier, more than double. These are trophy properties changing hands at a pace the market has not seen in the comparison period, and each one is a large amount of money moving into a small number of homes. When the most expensive homes in a city sell faster and for more, it pulls the whole luxury tier’s confidence up with it.

Who is buying explains a lot of it. Mainland Chinese buyers accounted for 72% of luxury primary-market deals in the third quarter — nearly three out of every four. In plain words, this surge is powered mainly by money arriving from outside the city, aimed at a supply of top-end homes that is scarce by nature. When outside money chases a small number of properties, prices can rise quickly even if the wider economy and the everyday housing market feel flat.

The lesson travels to Central Florida, and it cuts both ways. A headline about luxury prices jumping in one famous city is not a reading of what a house in Orlando, Kissimmee, or Sanford is worth — the top of one market moves on its own forces, including money arriving from abroad. What does apply here is the habit: watch where outside money is flowing, but judge your own home or your own purchase by local sold prices, local supply, and local days on market. That is the yardstick that decides real deals in Central Florida.

Source: CBRE data — reported November 10, 2026

LOCAL · Interest Rates

Orlando home values ease while homes still go under contract in about six weeks

November 8, 2026

Home values in Greater Orlando have eased a little without the market standing still. The typical home value was $383,302, down 1.4% from a year ago. In plain words, that is a gentle softening, not a slide: the typical home is worth slightly less than it was a year ago, after a long run of gains. This reading is Zillow’s typical-value measure, which is a different yardstick from the sold-price median in the Stellar MLS story above, so the two numbers will not match exactly — each story on this page credits its own source and its own measure.

Homes are still moving at a workable pace, and buyers have real choice. A home was taking about 41 days to go under contract — roughly six weeks from listing to an accepted offer. That is slower than the rush years, when good homes went in days, and it gives a buyer time to look, compare, and think. Choice is the other half of the picture: 16,482 homes were for sale, so a buyer who walks away from one home has many others to consider, and a seller is competing against a long list every single day.

Financing is still the heavy part of the monthly payment. The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate stood at 7.18% in November 2026. In plain words, at a rate like that, interest takes a large share of every monthly payment, and the same income supports a smaller loan than it did when rates were low. That is why price and rate have to be read together: a slightly lower price does not automatically mean a comfortable payment, and a buyer’s real budget is set by the payment, not by the headline price alone.

For a Central Florida seller, the plain reading is that a fairly priced home still moves — about six weeks to contract is a normal, healthy pace — but pricing has to respect the buyer’s payment math and the 16,000-plus other homes on offer. For a buyer or an investor, this is a market that rewards homework: time on the market is room to negotiate on price or terms, and any purchase should be run at today’s 7.18% rate, not at a lower rate someone hopes arrives later. A deal that only works if rates fall is a hope, not a deal.

Source: Zillow Research and Freddie Mac data via FRED — November 2026

UNITED STATES · Real Estate Economy

Big landlords are now selling more Central Florida homes than they buy

November 4, 2026

The largest landlords have flipped from buyers to net sellers in Central Florida. Invitation Homes, the biggest name in that group, sold 658 homes while buying 216 in the third quarter of 2026 — roughly three homes sold for every one bought. For years these companies were the competition a local family faced at every starter-home listing; that has reversed. When the biggest buyer in the room becomes a seller, the whole feel of the market for ordinary homes changes with it.

The listing numbers show the scale of the turn. Institutional listings more than doubled over the year, rising from 4,166 in February to 9,447 in July, according to Parcl Labs. And these homes are being priced to move, not to test the market: 54% of institutional listings carry a price cut, compared with 38.7% of all listings. In plain words, more than half of the homes the big landlords are selling have already had their price reduced at least once — a sign of sellers who would rather close than wait.

Federal law is part of the shift. The ROAD to Housing Act, in effect since July 11, 2026, bars investors who already own 350 or more single-family homes from buying more. In plain words, the very largest owners can no longer keep adding houses to their holdings, which removes their buying power from the market at the same moment many of them are listing what they already hold. Policy, not just price, is pushing these homes back toward the open market and toward individual buyers.

For Central Florida families, the hopeful reading is real: more starter homes coming back to the market means more chances to buy a home that, a few years ago, might have gone to a landlord’s portfolio instead. For an owner thinking of selling, the other side of the same story matters — your home may now compete with institutional listings on nearby streets, many of them already price-cut. That makes your own pricing, condition, and timing worth getting right from day one, ideally against the sold prices on your own street, not against the highest asking price you can find.

Source: Parcl Labs data and Invitation Homes results, reported by Homes in Orlando — November 4, 2026
